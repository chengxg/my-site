Pressure Leveling Sensor

Author: 仁泉之子

A DIY weigh-scale pressure leveling sensor for 3D printers. The weigh-scale pressure bed is currently one of the few reliable nozzle-endstop sensor solutions — alternatives like piezo vibration sensors or pressure-film sensors on the toolhead mount less stably and are less dependable, with a risk of crashing.

A browser-based visualization host page is provided — no installation needed, just open the URL: https://demo.chengxg.top/tiaoping-sensor/html/index.html

 

The hardware uses an STC32G8K64 clocked at 24 MHz. This MCU doesn't even need a crystal — its minimal system is extremely simple. Its ADC sampling rate reaches 800 kS/s. Although this chip uses the C251 architecture with weak compute, its ADC is considerably stronger than those of the ESP32/ESP8266, which is more than enough for this application.

All passives are 0805 capacitors and resistors — easy to solder, total cost around 10 RMB.

Video introduction:   https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1RZgFeTE9K/?spm_id_from=333.999.0.0&vd_source=80c1c161f08d9d188b6befe8e1a3d23a

When the ADC change stays below a threshold over a set interval, the bed is considered at rest and the reading is automatically set as the calibration (zero) value.

ADC data can be streamed to the page as fast as every 0.25 ms, letting you inspect the waveform during leveling to determine the optimal trigger force. The firmware is fully asynchronous and non-blocking, so even streaming at such a high rate does not affect leveling accuracy.

Communicating with the browser page requires a USB-to-serial module; the baud rate is 1M.

The module offers four gain settings — 50×, 200×, 1000×, 5000×. Just pick one channel according to your needs. Setting a channel's gain to 0 disables it; the fewer channels enabled, the higher the refresh rate. With all four channels enabled the refresh rate reaches 15 k/s; with a single channel, 30 k/s.

 

The baud rate is fairly high — you need a CH340-based USB-to-serial module to connect to this module. Modules based on other chips may fail to connect.

Because the endstop signal output is in series with a 100-ohm resistor, some mainboards may fail to trigger the endstop; the resistor needs to be reduced.

2024-10-07 update

The firmware has been ported to the STM32F072C8T6, clocked at 48 MHz. The framework code is generated by STM32CubeMX, and the development tool is PlatformIO in VS Code — easier to use, lower cost, better performance. The refresh rate is raised to 50 k/s, serial data now goes through a USB virtual COM port (no CH340 needed), the baud rate is freely adjustable, and the 200× gain channel has been dropped.

Firmware is flashed with STM32CubeProgrammer

 

 

 

----------------------- 2025-05-05 major update  — new-generation pressure leveling sensor--------------------

 

 

This full redesign keeps a single sampling channel, using two cascaded INA180A2 stages for a maximum gain of 2500×, balancing precision against data noise. A two-stage RC filter greatly reduces noise — ±4 counts under clean conditions and only ±10 with interference — allowing lower trigger thresholds. The software is also a major upgrade: higher performance and easier operation.

 

  • 1. Two cascaded INA180A2 op-amps plus an added inverting channel, so signal polarity no longer matters. Some sensors have large zero drift and the zero point can invert. The INA180A2 is also cheaper.
  • 2. PCB area greatly reduced, sized to match the pressure toolhead; a hub board adapts it to the pressure bed.
  • 3. Experimental support for a four-wire probe, similar to FLY's new ALPS pressure leveling: use the probe's activate_gcode and deactivate_gcode to actively control probe enabling, so probing only runs during Z probing — greatly reducing interference from XY moves. The trigger direction can be changed: with some sensors the reading decreases under load once installed, in which case you adjust the trigger direction.
  • 4. Brand-new browser-based host UI — good-looking, smooth, no installation required. Parameter adjustment is simpler with more options, including button-adjustable trigger thresholds. The overall UX logic is more user-friendly, and the ECharts chart is optimized to store up to 120 s of waveform data — up to 12 million data points at 100 k samples per second with real-time refreshing. It runs smoothly and is suitable for ultra-high-speed strain-gauge acquisition.
  • 5. Optimized firmware: trigger detection reaches 200 k/s and data output up to 100 k/s — far faster than the CS1237 solution. Serial communication runs at 2M baud, and the USB-to-serial chip is an AI8H2K12U with driver-free support on Windows 10/11.
  • 6. Data import/export added. Measured single-point probing accuracy is very high: at a probe speed of 30 mm/s the single-point accuracy is 0.003750, and at 5 mm/s 0.00125 is very likely. You can analyze the actual noise data for quantitative research — a DIY module reaching Bambu Lab's level is not a dream.
  • 7. Forces the endstop signal when readings drop below 50 or exceed 4000. A watchdog timer is added to prevent accidents from an MCU crash — stronger protection.
  • 8. The MCU is back to the STC32G8K64 in a TSSOP-20 package — easy to solder and cheap. STC-ISP sets the run frequency to 40 MHz. Fully register-level code, easily outperforming the library-based STM32F072C8T6, with better ADC precision too. The integrated AI8H2K12U USB-to-serial is cheaper and faster than a CH340 and won't blue-screen your PC, achieving 200 k/s refresh with full-bandwidth real-time streaming at 2M baud.
  • 9. A button is added for adjusting the trigger threshold, similar to the AIR001 approach  https://oshwhub.com/aoang/air001_bedprobe
  • 10. The web page is hosted on GitHub, which is slow to access from mainland China — you can download it and open it locally. Page address: https://demo.chengxg.top/tiaoping-sensor2/
  • 11. You can substitute other INA180 variants — INA180A1 (20×), INA180A3 (100×), INA180A4 (200×) — to compose other gains. Higher gain means more noise and more zero-drift sensitivity. The default combination is two INA180A2 at 50×50. You can also change the RC values between the two stages to alter sensitivity: smaller resistance gives higher sensitivity but more susceptibility to interference.
  • 12. TVS-protected; input voltage must not exceed 7V. The Type-C input has diode isolation, and USB power is isolated from the mainboard supply — no need to worry about your PC's safety.
  • 13. On first firmware download you must short the capacitor shown in the picture so the MCU resets before the program can be flashed. For subsequent downloads, use the "Restart MCU" button in the host software to reset.
  • 14. In normal operation LED1 blinks once per second. One sample is taken every 100 µs; when each sample stays within the zeroing deviation for 3 s, the module auto-zeros using the average of the samples, and LED1 blinks rapidly a few times to indicate zeroing. LED2 is the trigger status indicator.

----------------------- 2025-08-31 update  — new-generation pressure leveling sensor, CS1237 and LDC1612 versions--------------------

 

The CS1237 version parallel-connects multiple chips to speed up sampling and uses a REF303 voltage reference, so data noise is very small. Compared with the op-amp version it has higher sensitivity, better linearity and a wider measurement range — the only downside is a lower sampling rate. The highest Klipper probe precision is slightly below the op-amp version but still sufficient: 0.00375 is achievable. You can solder just one CS1237; the difference from two in parallel is small.

  • A filtering feature is added; enabling it effectively reduces data noise, at the cost of added latency.
  • A zeroing-time setting is added: within the configured number of seconds, when data fluctuation is below the configured zeroing deviation, it auto-zeros.
  • Data protection: when the absolute value of the reading is below 30 or above 10000, the data is considered abnormal and the endstop output is triggered.
  • If no data is read for more than 20 ms, the CS1237 chip is considered faulty and the endstop output is triggered.
  • If the reading stays unchanged for a long time, the CS1237 chip is also considered faulty and the endstop output is triggered.
  • When using multiple sensors in parallel on a pressure bed, connect each one individually and check that the readings agree. Pay attention to data polarity and the direction readings move under load — they must be consistent.
  • Don't rely on the marked mounting direction of load-cell sensors; go by the actual data. Avoid a state where the reading is negative at rest and positive under load.
  • Interference with pressure sensors is real. Heated-bed interference splits into PWM interference and AC interference. PWM interference can be solved by grounding the bed; AC interference is introduced by that grounding and requires coupling the DC supply's GND with the earth line. If the noise is small you can leave it alone.
  • PWM interference is easy to spot; AC interference only shows up when you unplug your laptop's power adapter — with the adapter plugged in, the laptop's USB port grounds the system. That's why some people see no noise with USB plugged in but noise once unplugged.
  • Data noise is below 8 with no interference, 20 with AC interference, 50 with PWM interference, and 30 with both AC and PWM interference. PWM interference alone clearly has the biggest impact — in short, grounding the heated bed solves the problem well.

 

The LDC1612 version implements a proximity-switch function: it can be used either as a contactless endstop (an ordinary proximity switch) or as a nozzle endstop (similar to IDM's touch function).
The nozzle endstop works by watching the data rise: when the nozzle touches the bed the data stops rising, and at that moment the endstop triggers. It demands a very clean nozzle and PEI sheet. It works, but it is not as reliable as pressure leveling — this version is exploratory only.

To use it, first place the coil near the nozzle, about 2 mm above the nozzle tip. Then move the bed to about 1 mm below the nozzle and take the current reading as the trigger threshold — that's the proximity-switch function. When the reading exceeds the trigger threshold, the endstop output triggers. In nozzle-endstop mode, the trigger fires when the reading is above the threshold and continues rising until it stops increasing or slightly decreases — that's when the nozzle is deemed to have touched the bed.

Mode control lets a single IO pin switch the module between nozzle triggering and contactless triggering — e.g. contactless endstop for mesh probing and leveling, nozzle endstop for Z homing.

 

----------------------- 2026-02-13 update  — new-generation pressure leveling sensor, CS1237 version + panelized hub board--------------------

1. The hub board is panelized with the pressure module and connected by wires in between. For pressure-bed use, connect them directly in parallel for easier wiring; for pressure-toolhead use, snap them apart to save space.

2. The CS1237 version now has a firmware whose readings can cross the zero point — particularly useful for sensors with an uncertain zero. Older versions work too.

3. This one is drawn in LCEDA Pro; the project files are provided as attachments.

4. Minor update: the module's mounting screw holes are no longer connected to GND; everything else is unchanged. The hub board gained a ground point connected to the board's GND through a capacitor and resistor. The hub board's XH2.54 terminals can be either SMD or through-hole. See the descriptions above for other details.
