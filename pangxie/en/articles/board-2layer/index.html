3D Printer Mainboard — STM32 High-Performance Mini 8-Axis Mainboard (2-Layer)

Author: 仁泉之子
  • 1. This is a mini 8-axis mainboard, 100 mm × 75 mm — a single 8 cm silent fan is enough for cooling.
  • 2. This is a protected board: the GPIOs to the drivers have over-voltage protection and can survive a direct 24V hit — meaning if one driver blows, the board doesn't burn out and the other drivers are fine. The mainboard supports automatic shutdown.
  • 3. 8 stepper drivers: the first 6 support standalone, UART, and SPI modes; the last two support UART mode.
  • 4. The core board consists of an STM32F446RCT6 plus an STM32F072C8T6 — the F446 is the main controller, and the F072 provides an independent CAN interface.
  • 5. The mainboard has 6 endstop inputs, three of them shared with sensorless homing, one accepting high-voltage input.
  • 6. 4 controlled fan channels, two of them supporting 24V and 12V.
  • 7. 2 thermistor channels; if that's not enough, driver slots can be repurposed to expand thermistor and fan channels.
  • 8. 2 hotend outputs — one high-power; both hotend outputs need soldered lead-out wires.
  • 9. The board integrates a CH334R USB hub chip: one port goes to a CH340N USB-to-serial output (can be used to connect a toolhead directly), one to the main STM32F446RCT6, one to the STM32F072C8T6 for CAN output, and one is a spare USB port for other devices.

 

  • Special circuit-protection design with an extremely large number of TVS diodes: the 24V, 12V, and 5V rails all have TVS protection, and GPIOs are protected by SRV05 quad ESD-protection TVS chips — each SRV05 protects 4 GPIOs (measured clamping voltage about 7V). The base board integrates an STC8G1K08A MCU (SOP-8) that monitors the GPIO pin voltages; when a GPIO voltage exceeds the threshold it immediately cuts the 24V and 5V rails for protection, and also opens a bleed MOS to discharge the 24V capacitors to ground, further limiting damage to the MCU.
  • With TMC2209 driver modules, the pin voltage reaches 4V when enabled. Boards whose drivers connect directly to the MCU are relatively easy to damage: MCU pins are usually 3.3V while the driver module's communication voltage is higher than 3.3V — the current is small so the impact is limited, but there is a real risk of burning the MCU. This design provides excellent protection against MCU pin over-voltage.
  • The STC8G1K08A MCU on the base board is compiled with TianwenBlock and flashed with STC-ISP; select a 24 MHz clock and the STC8G1K08A-SOP8 part.
  • The STM32F072C8T6 and STM32F446RCT6 on the core board are flashed with STM32CubeProgrammer in DFU mode.
  • STEP7 can be used to expand SPI communication.
  • STEP8 can be used to expand fans and thermistors.
  • Once the mainboard is soldered, you must flash a program into the STC8G1K08A before it will power on normally.
  • The driver expansion board includes a UART-to-RS232 module, a STEP7 SPI expansion module, and a STEP8 module with 2 thermistor and 2 fan channels.
  • How to test the 24V-direct-to-IO protection: use a dupont wire to connect the VCC 24V pin at the top-right of a driver slot, and plug the other end into any of the communication pins on the left side of the driver slot (STEP, DIR, EN, or SPI pins all work). The mainboard should immediately trigger shutdown. (Test without the core board installed first; only after that passes, retest with the core board in — if it burns, that's on you.)
  • The pin headers on the core board must be the 14 mm extended version, otherwise they won't be tall enough.
  • The 100 µF 35V SMD capacitors can be replaced with regular through-hole capacitors (removing the stand-off style) — much easier to solder.
  • The board set is split into a core board and a base board; both PCBs are 2-layer and sized under 10 cm to cutprototyping cost.
  • The standard edition of LCEDA only allows one board per project, so I put multiple boards into a single project — copy out whichever one you want and modify it separately.
  • If the board gets stuck and won't power on, some module is unstable at power-up and high voltage leaks into the IOs. You can tweak the code to skip over-voltage monitoring during power-on.
  • All SMD parts on the base board are on the back and all through-hole parts on the front — very convenient for repairs.

 

2024-11-17 update:

An STM32H7 core board is added — just use the STM32H750VET6, it's even cheaper. It has two onboard UART serial ports and one bridged CAN interface; otherwise it's nearly identical to the STM32F446 core board and shares the same base board.

 

2024-12-02 update: serial-communication toolhead added

With CAN toolheads all the rage, let's just use serial. It's built on the STM32F072C8T6 (an STM32F103C8T6 works too), flashing and Klipper communication over serial — say goodbye to hard-to-find CAN UUIDs and dropped connections. 

4 fans, 3 endstops, 2 thermistors, 1 chamber temp, 1 TMC2226 driver, 1 heater cartridge, 1 ADXL345 accelerometer. Slightly higher cost, but no difficulty soldering.
A perfect match for this mini 8-axis mainboard.

 

2025-01-22 update: layout optimization

1. Optimized base board layout and power management: the 24V and 5V rails now start independently — 5V first, then a soft-start for 24V. The STC8G1K08A code was updated and is incompatible with earlier versions.

2. Optimized the STM32H750 core board layout.

3. Optimized the serial toolhead layout — different shape and different terminal blocks.

 

2025-03-15 update: new version of the 8-axis base board, interfaces unchanged

1. The power-monitor MCU is upgraded from STC8G1K08A SOP-8 to STC32G8K64 TSSOP-20; set the download clock to 40 MHz.

2. STEP3/STEP4 support 48V high-voltage drivers. Use high-voltage drivers with care — on GPIO over-voltage the mainboard cannot actively cut the 48V rail.

3. A terminal is reserved to feed 24V into the core board, in preparation for the next core board.

4. The 8-axis base board supports power monitoring, with a soft-start for the 24V rail; LED-strip power also works.

5. A new web-based power-monitor UI with a modern look lets you clearly see 24V/5V fluctuation, with waveform output up to 10 kHz. Just attach a high-speed USB-to-serial adapter (CH340, CH343, etc.). Online at: https://demo.chengxg.top/mini8/

6. The board has been optimized to qualify for JLCPCB's free ENIG PCB prototyping.
